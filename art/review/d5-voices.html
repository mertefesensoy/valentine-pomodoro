How Pomo & Doro talk

Phase 1, D5. The babble the pair speak in while their lines write on in the tutorial: a few made-up syllables each, synthesised in SoundCraft from its built-in synths, with no samples, no human voice and no words. Please listen on your phone's speaker as well as on headphones.

Version 2: softer

You said version 1 was too high-pitched and could hurt if it came out of the blue. So both voices now use the soft chime voice of the app's other sounds instead of a square wave, and sit about an octave lower: the highest note is E5 (659 Hz), where version 1 reached C♯6 (1,109 Hz). Nothing is boosted around 2 to 3 kHz any more, where the ear is most sensitive; the syllables start more gently, carry a touch of the chimes' small reverb, and play 2 LU quieter. Their brightness (spectral centroid) went from 0.5–1.3 kHz to 0.34–0.68 kHz. The bouncy glides stay: they are the cute part.

Your sound-design notes, applied to version 2

Version 3 is gone: these are version 2's files, exactly. Your notes' main cure for a robotic babble is variation when each syllable plays, so that is what's new, and it changes nothing in the files:

  • New: a little variation on every syllable. Each one plays a tiny bit higher or lower (a random ±10 cents, a tenth of a semitone at most) and a touch louder or softer (±1.5 dB), drawn fresh every time, so a line never repeats exactly. You approved this: version 2 with variation is the way to go.
  • To try: more variation. You suggested going a bit further, as an experiment. The switch has two bigger settings, ±20 cents and ±2.0 dB, and ±30 cents and ±3.0 dB. Past about ±30 cents (a third of a semitone) a voice starts to sound out of tune, so these stop there.
  • Already in version 2: a soft, warm tone with nothing in the harsh 2–4 kHz band; soft starts and gentle tails; a small set of syllables, never the same one twice running; a syllable on every 3rd or 4th letter, resting at commas and full stops; and babble that lasts as long as the line does.
  • Not used: recording voices or animal sounds. The app's rule is that every sound is synthesised in SoundCraft, with no samples and never a human voice.

Pomo

Pomo

Higher and bouncier. The app's own chime voice, a soft sine with a little sparkle an octave up, springing up into each note in short syllables. A syllable on every 3rd letter, about 7.4 a second.

Doro

Doro

Lower and rounder. A soft sine with a gentle triangle at the same pitch, settling onto each note in longer syllables. A syllable on every 4th letter, about 5.6 a second.

The English line: 14 syllables over 3.8 s, at −20.0 LUFS. Syllables 7.1–7.7 KB of 25 KB, peaks −12.1 dBFS at most, brightness (spectral centroid) 339–578 Hz.

Pace

The tutorial didn't have a write-on speed yet, so this is the proposal, the same one the pose sheet plays.

WhatProposed
Write-onOne character every 45 ms: 22.2 a second, about adult reading speed, so the line keeps up with the eye
Pauses150 ms after a comma; 300 ms after a full stop, question or exclamation mark inside the line
SyllablesPomo on every 3rd letter (about 7.4 a second), Doro on every 4th (about 5.6); the count starts again after each pause, and the last syllable rises for a question and falls otherwise
A full lineThe longest allowed (90 characters in English, 110 in Turkish) takes about 4.1 s and 5.0 s plus pauses. A tap on the bubble finishes it at once

For you to decide

  1. The voices. Are they soft and pleasant now, and still cute: clearly not a person, Pomo higher and bouncier, Doro lower and rounder? Should either be softer still, lower, slower or a different pitch?
  2. The level. Is the babble at the right loudness next to focus start, or should it sit higher or lower?
  3. The pace. Is 22.2 characters a second right for the write-on, or should it be faster or slower?
  4. The variation. Keep the approved ±10 cents, or does ±20 or ±30 sound better? Your notes also suggest the babble change with the mood. Pomo & Doro already change face, so it could follow them: a little higher when happy or excited, and higher still when surprised. Want that tried?
