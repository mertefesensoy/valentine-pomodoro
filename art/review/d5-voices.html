How Pomo & Doro talk

Phase 1, D5. The babble the pair speak in while their lines write on in the tutorial: a few made-up syllables each, synthesised in SoundCraft from its built-in synths, with no samples, no human voice and no words. Please listen on your phone's speaker as well as on headphones.

Version 3: cuter, still soft

You liked version 2 better but found it robotic. Its voice was nearly a pure tone, and a pure tone has no overtones for a vowel to live in; every syllable also landed squarely on a note, at a steady tick, in a fixed order. Version 3 keeps the same low, soft register and changes the rest:

  • A vowel in every syllable. A fuller tone through a gentle filter that opens and closes, like a small "nya" or "mwo". The filter tops out around 1 kHz, still well under the 2–4 kHz band that hurt in version 1.
  • A cute pitch shape. Pomo springs up past each note and settles back ("pyon"); Doro rises softly and sighs down a semitone.
  • Speech rhythm. A syllable falls on each syllable of the words, not on every 3rd or 4th letter, so the timing breathes like talk.
  • It follows the words. Each word's vowel picks the syllable (and the mouth shape: wide for "a", small for "e" and "i", round for "o" and "u"), and the line drifts gently downward with a little randomness, as a voice does. A question still rises at the end.

Version 3's brightness (spectral centroid) is 0.36–0.73 kHz, against version 1's 0.5–1.3 kHz, and its level is version 2's.

Version 2, for the record:

You said version 1 was too high-pitched and could hurt if it came out of the blue. So both voices now use the soft chime voice of the app's other sounds instead of a square wave, and sit about an octave lower: the highest note is E5 (659 Hz), where version 1 reached C♯6 (1,109 Hz). Nothing is boosted around 2 to 3 kHz any more, where the ear is most sensitive; the syllables start more gently, carry a touch of the chimes' small reverb, and play 2 LU quieter. Their brightness (spectral centroid) went from 0.5–1.3 kHz to 0.34–0.68 kHz.

Pace

The tutorial didn't have a write-on speed yet, so this is the proposal, the same one the pose sheet plays.

WhatProposed
Write-onOne character every 45 ms: 22.2 a second, about adult reading speed, so the line keeps up with the eye
Pauses150 ms after a comma; 300 ms after a full stop, question or exclamation mark inside the line
SyllablesOne on each syllable of the text (each run of consonants and vowels in a word), merged when closer than 110 ms for Pomo or 150 ms for Doro; the word's vowel picks the syllable, its height drifts down across the line with a little randomness, and the last one rises for a question and falls otherwise
A full lineThe longest allowed (90 characters in English, 110 in Turkish) takes about 4.1 s and 5.0 s plus pauses. A tap on the bubble finishes it at once

For you to decide

  1. The voices. Are they soft and pleasant now, and still cute: clearly not a person, Pomo higher and bouncier, Doro lower and rounder? Should either be softer still, lower, slower or a different pitch?
  2. The level. Is the babble at the right loudness next to focus start, or should it sit higher or lower?
  3. The pace. Is 22.2 characters a second right for the write-on, or should it be faster or slower?
